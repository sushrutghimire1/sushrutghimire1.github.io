The gap

Search tells you a thing exists. It doesn't tell you what to do about it.

This is the entire product difference, on one festival, in one town.

What you can already find

Sandkerwa takes place in Bamberg's Sand district in late August. It is one of the largest folk festivals in Franconia and attracts many visitors each year.

What TripBrief sends

Go Thursday. Saturday night on Sandstraße is shoulder-to-shoulder and you won't enjoy it. Rooms are already 2–3× normal — book today, not next week.

How it works

No app. No account. One forwarded email.

The lowest-friction input in travel is a booking confirmation the user already has in their inbox.

1

They forward a confirmation

Hotel, flight, or rental. We read the city and dates off it. Nothing to install, nothing to fill in.

2

We do the local research

Local-language sources, municipal calendars, and where we can, a person who lives there. Hand-written today, progressively automated.

3

One email, early enough to matter

Delivered while dates and bookings can still change. That timing is the whole product.

Why this, why now

The data is already worth a lot — to everyone except the traveller.

The category is proven B2B PredictHQ sells event-impact data into Booking.com, Uber, Amazon and Lighthouse. The premise that "what's happening here on these dates" has hard economic value is not in question.
Nobody serves the demand side All of that intelligence flows to pricing engines and revenue managers. The traveller — the person the events actually happen to — receives none of it.
The long tail just got cheap Reading thousands of local-language municipal and parish calendars was a funded project three years ago. It is now a scraping job plus a model.
Most of it is rule-based Sandkerwa is the Thursday before the last Sunday in August. Encode the rule, not the date, and the calendar regenerates itself every year.

We're writing the first 100 briefs by hand.

Deliberately unscalable, on purpose. Doing it manually is how we learn which half of this is automatable — and which half is the moat.

  • Now · 100 hand-written briefs, sourced from travellers already asking for this in public forums
  • Test · did they change a booking, a date, or a plan
  • Then · automate the mechanical half, keep humans on the judgement
  • Open question · consumer subscription vs. distribution inside the booking flow

TripBrief is an early prototype. Sample brief content is illustrative and not fact-checked.
