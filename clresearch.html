Research Papers This Week
P1
SimCLR: A Simple Framework for Contrastive Learning (Chen et al., 2020)
Why: The canonical contrastive learning framework. Understand batch-level contrast as baseline for CML's trajectory-level contrast.
Extract: How NT-Xent loss works; importance of projection head; what "uniformity" and "alignment" mean geometrically.
P2
MoCo v2: Improved Baselines with Momentum Contrast (Chen et al., 2020)
Why: Memory bank + momentum encoder = first temporal/persistent contrast mechanism. CML extends this idea over the full training trajectory.
Extract: How queue decay affects representation quality; the momentum encoder as a form of temporal smoothing.
P3
BYOL: Bootstrap Your Own Latent (Grill et al., 2020)
Why: Challenges the assumption that negatives are necessary. Critical for CML's claim that structured contrast ≠ just negative mining.
Extract: Role of stop-gradient; what the predictor head does to prevent collapse; how it relates to EMA target networks.
P4
Memory Networks (Weston et al., 2014)
Why: Foundational paper for external memory in neural networks. Establishes vocabulary for memory-as-first-class-object, which CML requires.
Extract: Read/write memory operations; how memory enables temporal reasoning; limitations relevant to CML's design.
